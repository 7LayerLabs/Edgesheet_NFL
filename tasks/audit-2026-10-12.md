# Prediction audit, 2026-10-12 (Desktop PC)

What was checked: every locked call in git (web/data/archive/2026, weeks 4 to 6), the code that makes and grades
them, the backtests behind the published verdicts, the week 5 DraftKings projections, and outside research on how
to do better. Nothing in the app was changed. Scratch scripts: the session scratchpad (audit/score.js,
audit/dfs-grade.js); rerun them against a fresh archive to update the numbers.

## 2. Code audit findings (verified in code)

How the live side is made (projection.ts): Elo margin (home 48, 25 Elo a point) + 0.4 x unit-edge score (sum of 4
percentile gaps / 40) + availability points. Win prob = normal curve with SD 13.5. Side = model margin vs posted
margin, any gap (0.1 counts). Total = per-team 22.4 + pace x half the EPA edge, plus availability and weather.

1. **Bug: line movement is measured on a different call than the one graded.** archive.ts grades the final lock's
   side but computes CLV from the first lock's side (archive.ts:325, ledger.ts). When the side flips during the
   week (Ravens at Falcons: ATL first, BAL at the final lock; Lions at Panthers too), the ledger shows CLV for the
   other team. And because the final lock follows the line to kickoff, CLV against it is zero by design. The record
   has no fixed "posted" call to measure.
2. **The live model is not the model that was backtested.** The published "48% ATS on 1,139 games" is Elo plus the
   QB term only. The live side adds the unit-edge term (up to about 4 pts, in every game), non-QB availability (up to
   8 pts), questionable-QB blending, and depth-chart starters. None were replayed (backtest.mjs:203 says "unit-edge
   blend not replayed"). /index says "Every model change is tested on past seasons first", which isn't true for these.
3. **Score line uses the posted total, not the model total** (projection.ts:215). Chiefs at Raiders locked 26-22 (48)
   while the model total was 44.5 with an under lean.
4. **Every game gets a graded side, even at a 0.1 gap**, and an exact tie defaults to home (projection.ts:246).
   Chiefs at Raiders (0.1 gap) went in the record as an LV cover.
5. **Totals backtest leaks.** The base (22.4 a team) is the average of the same 959 games it's graded on, and the
   shrink (0.5) was picked on them too (backtest-total.mjs:151). The backtest also needs 2 games of data, while the
   live model makes totals after 1.
6. **Unit-edge grades have no baseline and double count.** gradeEdge uses fixed cutoffs (4.6 yards a carry =
   "played out") without asking how often any offense clears them; "run game" and "ground game" edges are graded on
   the same rushing line, so one result counts twice (archive.ts:221-243).
7. **QB backtest leaks a little.** k = 0.75 picked in-sample; replacement levels computed over all seasons including
   later ones; starter falls back to "most plays in this game" (backtest-qb.mjs:125, 159).
8. **Copy that isn't true:** "no gap size beat 52.4%" (qb.json: 4 to 6 pts hit 56.7% on 97 games, 3 to 4 pts hit
   40.1%; noise both ways, but the sentence is wrong). "Confidence high" only means both inputs exist (40 of 43 locks)
   and still prints in report.ts:86 and consensus.ts:148.
9. **Consensus counts Elo twice**: the EdgeSheet row is itself mostly Elo, so Elo is 2 of 4 votes; a 2-2 split goes
   home (consensus.ts:164). Never backtested.
10. **Lock timing:** lockPregame trusts a status computed at the start of the slate build, with no clock check at
    write time, so a lock can land seconds after kickoff. The final lock is whatever the last render saw, including
    a render where NWS or ESPN failed.
11. **DFS:** simulator correlations are fit (good) but in-sample; two defenses in one game should be -0.26
    correlated and the model gives about 0. DFS backtests count only players with a stat row (optimistic misses).
    The value-pick "beat" is above the price-mates' mean; DK points are right-skewed, so a random pick beats the mean
    less than half the time. dfs-slate.ts:90 always uses 0.75 to play for a questionable player.
12. **Small:** win prob uses SD 13.5 (the Elo RMSE is 12.9; the modern NFL is about 12.5 to 13); totals rounded three
    times before the 2.5 threshold; the "open" line is really nflverse's line at ingest.

Checked and clean: spread sign conventions everywhere (mkMarket, projection, archive, CLV, implied totals, ledger),
Elo is truly walk-forward, game backtests grade against closing lines and use no roster files, availability isn't
double counted against the market, departed.json doesn't overlap rosters.

Never validated in the live model: the unit-edge term, all non-QB availability values and caps, questionable-QB
blending, weather tilts, week 2 totals, live win-prob calibration, the confidence label, the consensus, and the gap
tiers as a betting signal.

## 3. Research: what would actually help

Benchmarks (nflverse 2022-25, 1,139 games, computed by the research pass): closing spread misses the margin by 9.54
on average, closing total misses by 10.2, the closing line's win prob scores a Brier of 0.2095, and simply picking
the closing favorite won 67.5% straight up. EdgeSheet's backtest: 64% winners, margin miss 9.95, 48% ATS. A true
52.4% picker going 48% on 1,139 games would be about a 1-in-1,000 result, so the current side model has no skill
against the close.

### Game model, well supported

1. **Start from the market and model only the difference.** Fit, walk-forward, result minus line against b x (model
   minus line). b is how much the model deserves; if its range includes 0, the model shouldn't flag gaps. nfelo (one
   of the best public models) does best at about 65% market weight and still only ties the close; in the Prediction
   Tracker's ~50 systems the Vegas line had the lowest miss in 10 of 11 seasons. Expect this to take EdgeSheet from
   about 48% to about 50% by stopping bad gaps, not to 55%.
   nfeloapp.com/analysis/using-market-regression-to-improve-prediction-accuracy-in-the-nfl/
2. **Real cover probabilities with key numbers.** Center on the line, SD about 12.5 to 13, and use the empirical
   margin distribution (2015-25: 14.8% of games land on 3, 8.7% on 7; half a point through 3 is worth about 8% of
   stake). Win prob from the spread fits better with a logistic than the normal curve.
   nfeloapp.com/analysis/margin-probabilities-from-nfl-spreads/
3. **Judge by line movement, not wins.** Separating 55% from 52.4% takes about 2,200 bets; CLV is roughly 10x less
   noisy, so about 50 frozen calls can show something. Positive CLV still loses unless it beats the vig (about a
   point off key numbers). Pre-register thresholds; walk-forward only.
4. **QB values from regressed ratings plus an empirical backup cost** (538's QB adjustment cut Brier from 0.224 to
   0.219; backup starts cost about 3.4 pts and the market moves about 3.6, so it's priced). Better winners and Brier,
   about zero ATS. Free: nfeloqb publishes qb_elos.csv.
5. **Replace rank-based unit edges with opponent-adjusted, regressed EPA per play** (ridge, long windows, defense and
   rushing down-weighted, preseason prior from market win totals). Ranks throw away how big the gap is. Plain point
   margin still predicts about as well, so expect 0.1 to 0.3 pts of miss at best.
6. Situational angles (division dogs 52.1%, off a bye 50.6%, Thursday road 50.4%, home dogs 50.5%) are priced.
   Home field 1.7 pts (2023 estimate) is close to the current 48 Elo (about 1.9).

### Game model, worth a pre-registered test

7. **Wind and totals.** Outdoor games with 10+ mph wind went under the close 58 to 59% in 2015-25 (n about 680), only
   52% in 2005-14. nflverse wind is the recorded game-time value (look-ahead), so it must be retested with forecasts
   that existed at bet time: Open-Meteo's previous-runs archive (free, from Jan 2024).
8. **Two or more starting linemen out**: about 3 pts against the close on 140 team-games (unvetted source, 1.4
   standard errors). A better replacement for the flat 0.4 pts a lineman than the current guess.
9. **Bet timing.** Models look far better against the open than the close (nfelo 2023: 62.9% vs open, 50.5% vs
   close). Any edge may only exist early in the week, so measure CLV from an early frozen call.
10. Not worth building: late injury news edges (they last seconds), touts' player point-value lists.

### Player and DFS, well supported

1. **Prop-implied DK points as the core input.** One careful 2024-25 study (about 7,000 player-weeks): PPR miss 4.96
   from props vs 5.54 from a 5-game average, better at every position; Vegas features were 40 to 52% of feature
   importance in a 2025 thesis. Props also re-price injuries on their own. Needs Odds API player props: about 16k
   credits a season live, about 50k to backtest 2023-25.
2. **Implied team totals** (total/2 plus or minus spread/2) to scale team volume and TDs. Free in nflverse games.csv.
3. **Calibration slope per position.** Public projections over-project top players (season slopes QB 0.67, RB 0.79,
   WR 0.85, TE 0.72). Matters for the value lists.
4. **Usage features with shrinkage:** target share, WOPR, snap share, red-zone share carry over; efficiency (TD rate,
   EPA per target) doesn't, which matches the existing efficiency backtest.
5. **Grade value picks with plus/minus vs salary-implied points** (FantasyLabs style), against a "salary only" pick.
   Showing +1 pt a pick takes about 260 picks; +0.5 takes about 1,050.
6. What good looks like: best public weekly MAE (standard pool, top 20 QB/TE, top 40 RB/WR) is about QB 6.2, RB 5.1,
   WR 4.9, TE 3.8. Report EdgeSheet on the same pool, per position, against salary-implied and prop-implied baselines.

### Player and DFS, weak or no evidence

Ownership leverage (no free ownership data), exact stack correlations (direction holds; numbers are stale), weekly
prop unders (only season-long evidence), LLM news edges against the betting market (markets react fast; any edge is
more likely against DFS ownership and late swaps).
